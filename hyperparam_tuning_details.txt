\section{Hyperparameter Tuning Strategy}

We optimize the standard SVM parameters ($C$ and $\gamma$), alongside a custom sample-wise weighting parameter, using a dynamic, variance-aware search strategy. Rather than evaluating a static grid, our algorithm iteratively expands to escape performance plateaus and progressively halves the search step to converge on the optimal parameters over four refinement rounds.

### 1. Dynamic Logarithmic Search for Standard Parameters ($C$ and $\gamma$)

Because $C$ and $\gamma$ are highly sensitive to orders of magnitude, we search their space logarithmically. Let $\Delta$ represent the common difference of the exponents. For both $C$ and $\gamma$, we execute the following sequence for a total of 3 iterations:

1. **Initialization:** Initialization: We define a base exponent step size $\Delta = 1$ and evaluate an initial search space of $\{10^{-1}, 10^0, 10^1\}$.
2. **Variance Check (Plateau Escape):** We calculate the standard deviation, $\delta$, of the validation scores across the current search space. If the scores are virtually flat ($\delta < 0.01$), the search space is too narrow or poorly positioned. We recursively zoom out by extending the grid with the current $\Delta$ (e.g., expanding to evaluate $10^{-2}$ and $10^2$) until the variance condition $\delta \ge 0.01$ is satisfied. We do this a maximum of three times. 
3. **Directional Boundary Expansion:** Once $\delta \ge 0.01$, we identify the parameter yielding the highest validation score. If this best-performing value lies at the absolute leftmost or rightmost boundary of the current search space, we continue expanding the grid in that specific direction by steps of $\Delta$ until the validation score ceases to increase.
4. **Refinement (Zoom-in):** Once the optimal value is bounded (i.e., it is strictly internal, or directional expansion has terminated), we center a new search space around this best value. We update the step size by halving it ($\Delta \leftarrow \Delta/2$) and define the new local neighborhood for the next iteration.

This entire 4-step process is repeated for exactly 3 refinement rounds, systematically zeroing in on the optimal logarithmic value.

### 2. Dynamic Linear Search for Sample-wise Weights

To handle class imbalance, we apply a sample-wise weighting strategy to the SVM training. In the dual formulation, the upper bound $C$ on the Lagrange multiplier $\alpha_i$ is reweighed sample-wise to $\alpha_i \le C \times r_i$. We assign distinct weights to the minority (fold-switching) and majority (no-switch) samples using $r_+$ and $r_-$, respectively.

These weights are governed by a single structural parameter $a$, defined as:


$$a = \frac{1}{2r_+} = 1-\frac{1}{2r_-}$$

If $n_+$ out of $n$ total training samples belong to the minority class, we anchor our search around a theoretical baseline $\Bar{a} = \frac{n_+}{n}$.

We apply the exact same 4-round dynamic algorithm used for $C$ and $\gamma$ to optimize $a$, but operate in a **linear space** rather than an exponential one. Let $\Delta_a$ represent the linear step size.

1. We initialize $\Delta_a = \Bar{a}/2$ and evaluate the starting space $\{\Bar{a} - \Delta_a, \Bar{a}, \Bar{a} + \Delta_a\}$ (which corresponds to $\{\Bar{a}/2, \Bar{a}, 3\Bar{a}/2\}$).
2. If the validation score standard deviation $\delta < 0.01$, we zoom out linearly by steps of $\Delta_a$ until $\delta \ge 0.01$ (max 3 times).
3. If the best value sits at a boundary, we expand in that direction by $\Delta_a$ until the score stops increasing.
4. We then center on the best value, halve the linear step size ($\Delta_a \leftarrow \Delta_a/2$), and repeat for 3 rounds.

Throughout this dynamic search for $a$, we strictly enforce the mathematical boundary $0 < a < 1$ during any expansion phase to guarantee that the majority class weight $r_-$ remains positive and valid.
